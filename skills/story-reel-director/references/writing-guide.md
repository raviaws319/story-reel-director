# Writing guide (all modes)

## Pacing

| Length | Scenes | Words (story ≈ 2.6 w/s) | Words (kids ≈ 2.0 w/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 4–5 | 70–80 | 55–60 |
| 45 s | 5–6 | 105–115 | 85–90 |
| 60 s | 6–8 | 140–155 | 110–120 |
| 90 s | 8–11 | 210–230 | 170–180 |
| 3 min | 15–20 | 430–470 | 340–360 |
| 10 min | 50–70 | 1450–1550 | — |

One beat = one idea, usually **1–2 sentences (4–9 seconds)**. In the illustrated engine a beat is a scene with one image; in the acted engine a scene is a location holding several beats (the scene counts above are beats). The very first scene of a short is shorter (3–5 s).

## The hook (first 3 seconds)

The first sentence decides whether people keep watching. Use one of:

- **Shocking fact or number**: "For four years, he watered dirt."
- **Open loop**: "This one mistake ruined the Titanic, and it wasn't the iceberg."
- **Direct promise**: "This one habit doubled my savings in a year."
- **Question that hurts a little**: "Why do smart people stay broke?"
- **In-media-res**: start in the middle of the action, explain later.

Never open with "Hi guys", "In this video", the channel name or a slow introduction. Put a short `title` card on the hook scene (2–5 words that are *not* identical to the narration).

## Narration style

- Write for the ear: short sentences, everyday words, one idea per sentence.
- Use concrete pictures ("ninety feet in six weeks"), not abstractions ("grew a lot").
- Vary rhythm: a long sentence, then a short one. Like this.
- Use punctuation to create pauses; the voice and the captions both follow it.
- Write numbers the way they are spoken when it matters ("ninety feet", "two minutes").
- No stage directions, emojis or hashtags inside narration.

## Ending

- Story: a payoff line people want to share, then a soft call to action on an `end` card.
- Kids: a gentle closing and the moral (moral card required).

## Dialogue (acted engine)

Let characters speak. A story told half in dialogue feels alive: the narrator sets the scene and bridges time, characters say what they feel and want. Keep character lines short (under 12 words), give each character a way of speaking, and never have a character narrate their own action.

## Visual descriptions (illustrated engine: image_prompt)

Describe **what is in the frame**, not the style: subject, action, setting, camera (close-up, wide, overhead, low angle), light and mood. The runtime adds the style, the character looks and the "no text" rules. Keep each prompt to 1–2 sentences.
Recurring characters: define them once in `characters` with a precise look (colors, clothing, distinctive features) and refer to them by name in each prompt.
Never ask the image model for readable text, logos or brand packaging; on-screen words come from cards and captions.
