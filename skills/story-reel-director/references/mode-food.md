# Mode: Food Videos

Three formats; pick one from the request:

## 1. Recipe (default)

**Hook (finished dish) → Ingredients → Steps → Serve**

- Hook scene: the finished dish, a promise (time, taste, ease) and a `title` card.
- One `ingredients` card scene listing 4–8 items with quantities, kept short ("2 eggs", "1/2 onion, chopped"). The narration lists them naturally; it does not read every quantity if that sounds robotic.
- Steps: one scene per step with a `step` card (`number` + 3–6 word `text`). Combine tiny steps; 3–6 steps total for a short.
- Use a `timer` card (`minutes`, `label`) when waiting matters (baking, resting, simmering).
- Last scene: the serving shot with an `end` card ("Save this recipe", "Dinner in 15 minutes").
- Give quantities and times that actually work. Cooking temperatures in °C with °F in brackets when mentioned. Mention food safety where it matters (cook chicken through, let oil cool).

## 2. Food story

History, origin or culture of a dish ("How biryani travelled to India"). Use the Story Narrator arc and cards (`fact`, `end`) with food styles. Same accuracy rule as story mode: well-known facts only, legends marked as legends.

## 3. Food facts / tips list

"5 kitchen hacks", "Foods you store wrong". One item per scene with a `fact` card; the hook promises the payoff of the list.

## Visuals

- `warm-kitchen` (default): appetizing photo-real food shots. Describe angle (overhead flat lay, 45°, close-up), texture (crispy, glossy, steaming) and light.
- `flat-food`: playful flat illustrations, good for tips and kids' recipes.
- Avoid brand packaging and readable labels in prompts.

## Narration

Energetic but warm (+4–5 % rate). Use sensory words: sizzle, golden, crispy, fluffy, melt. Keep steps imperative and short: "Crack the eggs. Whisk until frothy."
